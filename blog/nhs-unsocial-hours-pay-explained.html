NHS Unsocial Hours Pay Explained 2026/27: Saturday, Sunday & Night Rates

By Sadeem · Last updated: 2 August 2026

For a huge number of NHS staff, the number on your contract is only part of the story — shift work, weekends and nights bring unsocial hours enhancements that can add a meaningful amount to your take home pay. This guide explains exactly how the enhancement rates work, walks through a real worked example, and links to a free calculator that works out your own numbers.

The NHS Unsocial Hours Enhancement Rates

Under Agenda for Change, three enhancement rates apply to hours actually worked in unsocial periods:

Period Enhancement
Saturday Time plus 30%
Sunday Time plus 60%
Weekday nights (8pm–6am) Time plus 30%

These enhancements are paid on top of your basic hourly rate for the specific hours you work in those periods — they don't replace your normal pay, they add to it. Bank holiday and on-call arrangements vary between trusts and roles and aren't covered by these three standard rates, so check your local terms if those apply to you.

How Your Basic Hourly Rate Is Worked Out

To calculate an enhancement, you first need your basic hourly rate. This is your annual gross salary divided by the number of weeks in a year (52.143), divided again by your contracted weekly hours — 37.5 hours for a standard NHS full-time week.

For example, a Band 5 nurse on the 2026/27 entry salary of £32,073 working a standard 37.5-hour week has a basic hourly rate of:

£32,073 ÷ 52.143 ÷ 37.5 = £16.40 per hour

Worked Example: A Typical Shift Pattern

Let's apply that hourly rate to a realistic weekly pattern: 7.5 hours on a Saturday, 7.5 hours on a Sunday, and 15 hours of weekday nights.

Enhancement Hours Rate Extra Pay
Saturday 7.5 +30% £36.91
Sunday 7.5 +60% £73.81
Weekday nights 15 +30% £73.81
Total per week £184.53

If that pattern repeated every week of the year, it would add roughly £9,622 to gross pay before annual leave is accounted for — a substantial uplift on top of the £32,073 basic salary. Because enhancements are taxed, NI'd and pensioned in the normal way alongside the rest of your income, the actual increase in take home pay is smaller than the gross figure, which is exactly what our NHS Unsocial Hours Calculator works out for you automatically.

Are Unsocial Hours Enhancements Pensionable?

Yes. Unsocial hours enhancements count as pensionable pay under the NHS Pension Scheme, meaning they increase both your pension contributions and the pension benefits you build up. This is one of the reasons regular unsocial hours working can meaningfully increase your total NHS pension over a career, not just your monthly pay.

It's also worth noting that a higher gross salary from enhancements can occasionally push you into a higher pension contribution tier for that period — our calculator accounts for this automatically when it estimates your take home pay.

Why the Take Home Uplift Is Smaller Than the Gross Uplift

A common source of confusion is expecting enhancement pay to arrive pound-for-pound in your bank account. In reality, unsocial hours pay is added to your gross salary and then taxed and NI'd like any other earnings, and it may also push a larger share of your income into a higher NHS pension contribution tier. For higher earners, a large enough enhancement could even tip a portion of pay into the 40% higher rate tax band. None of this makes unsocial hours work less worthwhile — it just means the number on your payslip will be lower than the headline enhancement total.
