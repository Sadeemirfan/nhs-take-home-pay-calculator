NHS Band 6 & 7 Salary After Tax: Senior Staff Take Home Pay

If you are working at a senior level in the NHS — or are about to step up from a junior role — understanding your NHS Band 6 salary after tax and NHS Band 7 take home pay is essential for financial planning. These two bands cover a wide range of highly skilled clinical and non-clinical professionals, including senior nurses, specialist practitioners, team leaders, and advanced allied health professionals.

In this guide, we break down exactly what you can expect to take home each month at both Band 6 and Band 7, including income tax, National Insurance (NI), and NHS pension contributions — plus what changes if you work in London.

NHS Band 6 and Band 7 Pay Scales at a Glance

Under the 2026/27 Agenda for Change pay framework, the pay ranges for these two senior bands are as follows:

  • Band 6: £39,959 to £48,117 per year
  • Band 7: £49,387 to £56,515 per year

Progression through each band is typically annual and based on satisfactory performance. It can take around four to five years to move from the entry point to the top of a band, depending on your employer and role.

How Your NHS Pay Is Taxed

Before we look at the numbers, here is a quick summary of the deductions that apply to all NHS employees in England:

  • Income Tax: You receive a personal allowance of £12,570 before any tax is due. Earnings above this are taxed at 20% (basic rate) up to £50,270, and at 40% (higher rate) above that.
  • National Insurance: You pay 8% NI on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% on anything above £50,270.
  • NHS Pension: Contributions are tiered by salary. The higher your pay, the higher the percentage you contribute. Crucially, pension contributions are taken from gross pay, which reduces your taxable income.

The NHS pension contribution tier for Band 6 entry pay of £39,959 falls within the 9.8% pension tier (covering pensionable pay from £35,156 to £52,778 under 2026/27 Agenda for Change tiers). At the top of Band 6 (£48,117), contributions remain at 9.8%, as this still falls within the same tier.

Band 7 is where many senior nurse salary earners sit, alongside advanced practitioners, team managers, and specialist allied health professionals. At £49,387, Band 7 entry pay falls in the 9.8% NHS pension tier. Top-of-band earners at £56,515 move into the 10.7% tier and cross the £50,270 higher-rate tax threshold, meaning a portion of their income is taxed at 40%.

London Weighting: What Difference Does It Make?

NHS staff working in London receive a cost-of-living supplement on top of their standard Agenda for Change salary. There are two tiers:

  • Inner London: A supplement of 20% of basic salary (with a minimum and maximum threshold)
  • Outer London: A supplement of 15% of basic salary

For a Band 6 nurse on entry pay of £39,959, inner London weighting would add approximately £7,992 to their gross salary, bringing it to around £47,951. For a Band 7 professional on £49,387, the same 20% supplement adds £9,877, giving a gross of approximately £59,264 — which would push them into higher-rate tax territory. Outer London at 15% is less dramatic but still meaningfully increases both gross pay and the taxes and pension contributions applied.

Because London supplements are treated as pensionable pay, your pension contributions also increase accordingly, which both raises the cost and boosts your eventual pension benefits.

Career Progression from Band 6 to Band 7

The move from Band 6 to Band 7 is one of the most significant steps in a clinical career. It typically involves taking on greater autonomy, extended leadership responsibilities, or a specialist or advanced clinical role. Common Band 7 roles include:

  • Senior ward sisters and charge nurses
  • Advanced clinical practitioners (ACPs)
  • Specialist physiotherapists, occupational therapists, and dietitians
  • Clinical nurse specialists (CNS)
  • Healthcare managers and service leads

From a financial perspective, the promotion is clearly worthwhile — but it is worth noting that pension tiers can rise as you progress through Band 7 (up to 10.7% at the top, versus 9.8% for most of Band 6), which slightly reduces the immediate net gain. You are, however, building a significantly more valuable pension for retirement as a result.

Many NHS professionals also use Band 6 as a platform to develop portfolios of evidence, complete master-level qualifications, and demonstrate leadership competencies required for Band 7 job applications. Average time at Band 6 before promotion varies widely but is commonly two to five years.

Summary

Whether you are a senior nurse evaluating a promotion, an allied health professional exploring Band 6 and 7 opportunities, or simply planning your finances, understanding your true take home pay is crucial. Key takeaways from this guide:

  • Band 6 take home pay ranges from approximately £2,365 to £2,787 per month
  • Band 7 take home pay ranges from approximately £2,853 to £3,107 per month
  • Pension contributions stay at 9.8% for most of Band 6 and entry-level Band 7, rising to 10.7% at the top of Band 7
  • London weighting adds meaningfully to gross and net pay but also increases deductions
  • The step from Band 6 to Band 7 is financially rewarding and builds a more valuable pension

Note: All figures are estimates based on the 2026/27 tax year, standard personal allowance of £12,570, and Agenda for Change pay rates. Tax rules and pay scales are subject to change. Always refer to your payslip or speak to your employer for exact figures.
