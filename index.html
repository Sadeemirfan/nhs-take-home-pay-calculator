Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about NHS pay, tax deductions and pension contributions.

Your take home pay is calculated by starting with your gross annual salary, then deducting Income Tax (based on your personal allowance and tax bands), National Insurance contributions, NHS Pension contributions (if applicable), and any student loan repayments. The remaining amount is your net take home pay. This calculator uses the 2025/26 HMRC tax rates and the current NHS Pension tiered contribution structure.

NHS Pension contributions are tiered based on your pensionable pay. These contributions are deducted before tax, reducing your taxable income and providing valuable tax relief on your pension savings.

Scotland has its own income tax rates set by the Scottish Parliament, which differ from the rest of the UK. For 2025/26, Scotland uses six tax bands: Starter (19%), Basic (20%), Intermediate (21%), Higher (42%), Advanced (45%) and Top (48%). In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, there are three main bands: Basic (20%), Higher (40%) and Additional (45%). Higher earners in Scotland generally pay more income tax than their counterparts elsewhere in the UK.

Postgraduate Loan repayments are 6% above Â£21,000. You only repay while your income is above the threshold, and repayments do not attract interest relief in the same way as pension contributions.

Salary sacrifice is an arrangement where you give up part of your salary in exchange for a non-cash benefit, such as additional pension contributions (AVCs), a cycle to work scheme bike, or an electric car lease. Because your contractual salary is reduced, you pay less income tax and National Insurance on the sacrificed amount, making it a tax-efficient way to receive benefits. However, it may affect your entitlement to certain state benefits and mortgage calculations, so consider your circumstances carefully.
